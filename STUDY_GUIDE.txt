STUDY GUIDE FOR SHADMAN
Internship Research Prime: preprocessing as a research problem

Use this guide alongside the scripts, audit tables and measured results. Your aim
is to explain a decision, show the evidence behind it, and state its limits. A
long notebook or a high accuracy number is not sufficient evidence of good work.

THE QUESTION YOU SHOULD BE ABLE TO STATE

Can source-reported Salmonella antibiotic resistance be predicted from isolate
metadata and existing AMRFinderPlus annotations, and how do missing-value handling,
categorical encoding and the way related isolates are split affect that estimate?

The primary task is ampicillin resistant versus susceptible. Tetracycline and
ciprofloxacin provide secondary tasks. Nonbinary and ambiguous labels are kept in
the audit trail. The work compares preprocessing choices while holding the main
classifier fixed. It does not discover genes from raw sequences, establish causal
mechanisms, estimate population prevalence or demonstrate clinical usefulness.

The source contains 142,338 assays, 9,977 assembled isolates and 9,976 BioSamples.
Say all three counts with their units. A single isolate tested against 15 drugs
contributes 15 assay rows, not 15 independent isolates. One BioSample has two
assembly records; it is quarantined from the primary feature table. Use measured
processed counts from results/preprocessing_summary.json for the final cohorts.

 10. Inspect exact_duplicate_assays.csv.gz and phenotype_pair_quarantine.csv.gz.

DAY 2: build leakage-safe missing-value and encoding pipelines

Morning: missingness and targets
  1. Read the measured feature-missingness table. Distinguish missing source type,
     missing host, missing collection year and missing numeric assembly quality.
     Verify actual counts; the numeric quality fields are complete in this frozen
     source, so do not claim substantial missingness in them.
  2. Explain why a missing target is never replaced with the most common class.
     An untested antibiotic is unknown, not susceptible.
  3. Inspect the real inter-platform conflict for piperacillin-tazobactam. Source
     phenotype disagreement remains unresolved even if one label is more common
     elsewhere. Explain what additional laboratory evidence would resolve it.
  4. Explain how completed zero-hit genotyping differs from unavailable analysis.
     All raw isolates here have amrfinderplus_applied=1. Missing-analysis handling
     is a supported policy tested with fixtures, not a frequent observed problem.

Afternoon: src/model_study.py
  5. Locate feature_columns and the forbidden-feature check. Write X and y on paper.
     Explain why MIC and number_drugs_resistant must be absent from X even if they
     predict resistance extremely well.
  6. Inspect build_pipeline. Numeric imputation and scaling, categorical imputation
     and encoding, gene vocabulary learning and logistic regression are fitted
     together on training data. Cross-validation fits a fresh pipeline per fold.
  7. Compare median, median_flags and iterative_flags. Indicators preserve the fact
     that a value was missing; iterative imputation estimates numeric relationships.
     Neither method proves that data are missing at random or recovers the truth.
  8. Compare ordinal and one-hot encoding. Inspect encoding_maps.json rather than
     assuming the mapping order. Both encoding arms retain the same training
     category set without rare-category pooling. Unseen categories never change the vocabulary.
  9. Inspect GeneEncoder. A row can contain multiple annotations, so one integer per
     isolate would discard information. Multi-hot columns retain symbol=status
     tokens. A token appearing twice is presence, not measured gene copy number.
 10. Explain how unknown categories and unknown genes behave at inference. Check
     summary.json for the measured unseen-gene counts and inference checks.

Day 2 deliverable: a diagram of the fitted pipeline plus two small worked feature
encodings and a written explanation of one-hot versus ordinal assumptions. Use
actual training maps when naming any final feature columns or integer codes.

DAY 3: evaluate honestly, demonstrate reproducibility and defend the result

Morning: splits, experiments and metrics
  1. Read results/model_study/split_manifest.csv.gz. Verify no recorded component
     occurs in train and validation/test simultaneously. A 60/20/20 group split
     does not guarantee exactly 60/20/20 isolate counts when cluster sizes vary.
  2. Read the experiment protocol in summary.json before selecting rows from
     metrics.csv. The fixed main model is onehot_median_flags, with ampicillin as
     primary. The other preprocessing combinations are controlled comparisons.
  3. Compare metadata_only, genes_only and combined models. Improvements can reflect
     known resistance annotations, lineage, collection patterns or source bias.
     They do not show that a metadata association causes biological resistance.
  4. Interpret average precision against test resistance frequency; inspect
     resistance recall and precision together. Then inspect balanced accuracy,
     ROC AUC, Brier score, calibration and confusion counts. State the threshold.
  5. Read bootstrap_ci.csv and paired_differences.csv. The same resampled groups are
     used for model comparisons. Intervals describe test-group variation conditional
     on fitted models; they do not include all possible training uncertainty.
  6. Inspect cv_metrics.csv and split_sensitivities.csv. BioSample-random splitting
     can leave clonal relatives on both sides. Project holdout probes a different
     generalization question and may have very uneven class support.

Afternoon: reproducibility and viva practice
  7. Run src/validate_preprocessing.py and inspect preprocessing_validation.json.
     Explain an independently hand-calculated fixture, a source integrity check

  8. Read streaming_benchmark.json. Compare chunk sizes only after verifying equal

  9. Rerun using bash setup_wsl.sh && bash run_wsl.sh. Record package versions and

 10. Prepare a five-minute demonstration: question -> source units -> real cleaning
     case -> split/encoding policy -> measured comparison -> remaining uncertainty.

Day 3 deliverable: a short presentation or verbal demonstration containing one
provenance check, one difficult cleaning example, one encoding example and one
carefully interpreted measured result. No result needs to be impressive to be
scientifically useful; the evidence must support what you say.

WORKED EXAMPLES

A. Censoring
  Source: MIC <=4 mg/L.
  Meaning: the value is in (0,4], subject to the assay's reporting conventions.
  Store: lower=0, upper=4, lower_closed=False, upper_closed=True, censored=True.
  Incorrect shortcut: treat the value as observed exactly 4 and fit ordinary
  regression without acknowledging censoring.

  Source: MIC >8 mg/L.
  Store: lower=8, lower_closed=False, upper=None (unbounded), upper_closed=False.
  The boundary 8 is not the measured concentration.

B. Combination measurements
  Source: piperacillin-tazobactam >64/4.
  Retain the primary concentration/comparator and the reported secondary
  concentration. Do not divide 64 by 4, and do not invent a second comparator.
  In this project the measurement table is audited; MIC values are excluded from
  resistance predictors because they are part of how source phenotypes arise.

C. Structural missingness
  Source: a disk-diffusion result in mm, with no MIC in mg/L.
  A missing MIC is expected for that assay. Filling MIC with the median would
  fabricate another kind of test. Retain the disk result separately. It is not
  interchangeable with an isolate metadata feature that happens to be unrecorded.

D. Label encoding
  Source susceptible -> y=0; source resistant -> y=1.
  Intermediate -> excluded from the primary binary task and retained in audit.
  Nonsusceptible -> retained as its own source category; it is not silently R.
  Not defined, missing and contradictory labels -> no invented binary target.
  Class encoding defines the question. A different definition requires a separate
  protocol and sensitivity analysis, not a hidden replacement in the same run.

E. Nominal categories
  Toy categories: animal, food, human. Codes 0,1,2 are labels, not biological
  distances. A linear model using those codes imposes a numeric trend. One-hot
  encoding gives a separate column per represented category. The actual fitted
  vocabulary comes only from training data and is saved for inspection.

F. Gene annotations
  Example token: blaTEM-1=COMPLETE. Another token with a partial or altered-call
  status is not silently upgraded to COMPLETE. Parentheses, apostrophes and slash
  characters inside gene names are meaningful. Distinct calls use multiple
  presence columns; copy number cannot be recovered from this representation.

G. Split leakage
  Suppose isolates A and B share a recorded SNP cluster. If A is in training and B
  in test, a model may perform well by exploiting near-identical lineage features.
  Keep their connected group together. Isolates with missing cluster links fall
  back to BioSample grouping; absence of a link is not proof of unrelatedness.

DEEPER VIVA QUESTIONS AND WHAT A GOOD ANSWER MUST INCLUDE

1. Why is 142,338 not the effective independent sample size?
   It counts assays. Repeated drugs, repeated measurements and related isolates
   create dependence. Quote BioSample/isolate counts, then per-drug eligible counts
   and recorded group counts from actual outputs.

2. Why not remove every repeated BioSample row?
   Different antibiotics are useful long-format observations. Exact duplicate
   assays can be flagged separately. Repeated methods can provide discordant
   evidence that should be audited rather than deleted indiscriminately.

3. Why not use the majority label for a conflict?
   Multiple records are not necessarily independent votes. Method/platform and
   testing-standard differences may matter. Quarantine avoids inventing ground truth.

4. Why not use LabelEncoder on every feature column?
   It is suitable for class labels, not generally nominal multi-category predictors
   with a linear model. Explain artificial order, training-only vocabularies and
   unknown-category behavior. Explicit y mapping is deterministic and auditable.

5. Can missing host information be called missing at random?
   Not from these data alone. Missingness may depend on submitter, surveillance
   purpose or unobserved features. An indicator measures recordedness; it does not
   identify a missingness mechanism or remove selection bias.

6. Is iterative imputation automatically better than median imputation?
   No. It assumes useful relationships and adequate support. Some numerical fields
   are complete here. Inspect validation/test comparisons and uncertainty; do not
   claim gains unless actual outputs support them. This is single imputation.

7. Why retain quality status in gene tokens?
   COMPLETE, POINT, MISTRANSLATION and partial calls have different annotation
   meanings. Preserving them avoids pretending every detection has identical
   evidence. This can increase sparsity; a future status-collapsing experiment
   should be explicit and fitted/evaluated without test-set selection.

8. Is a high accuracy enough for ciprofloxacin?
   A mostly susceptible sample can yield high accuracy by predicting susceptibility.
   Inspect resistant support, resistance recall, precision, AP and confusion counts.
   Check whether group splitting leaves enough cases of each class.

9. What does the bootstrap interval omit?
   The current bootstrap resamples test groups while models remain fixed. It does
   not refit training models, resolve source-label error, remove batch effects or
   account for every possible split. State that scope when citing an interval.

10. Why compare BioProject holdout with SNP-component holdout?
    They probe different dependence structures. Project holdout can expose
    submitter/batch shifts; SNP holdout addresses recorded clonal similarity. Neither
    automatically constitutes geographic, temporal or external clinical validation.

11. Why not recompute all S/I/R labels from MIC?
    Reliable reinterpretation requires organism/drug-specific breakpoints, standard
    editions, methods and combination semantics. Edition information is incomplete.
    This study keeps source labels and does not invent breakpoint harmonization.

12. How would Spark improve this particular project?
    It may not: this frozen source fits local processing and Spark adds overhead.
    A defensible extension would compare identical transformations on a genuinely
    larger source, measuring shuffle, memory, wall time and equivalent outputs.
    Duplicating rows to claim biological scale would not add evidence.

13. Does grouping eliminate all leakage?
    It prevents overlap in recorded components. Missing links, broader lineage,
    reference-database annotations and source-specific effects remain. Explain the
    explicit feature exclusion list and the limits of the available cluster data.

14. What is your own research contribution here?
    Understanding and defending the data model, inspecting actual edge cases,
    specifying cleaning/encoding policies, reproducing controlled experiments and
    honestly interpreting the evidence. AI-assisted implementation is disclosed;
    generating files alone is not equivalent to understanding or original discovery.

A DEFENSIBLE FIVE-MINUTE EXPLANATION

First minute: describe the new NCBI Salmonella source and distinguish assays,
isolates and BioSamples. State the prediction target and study limits.

Second minute: show one raw conflict, one censored MIC and one structural-missing
case. Locate each audit entry and explain the rule without claiming ground truth.

Third minute: show the training-only pipeline. Explain binary target mapping,
one-hot versus ordinal predictors, gene multi-hot tokens and missingness handling.

Fourth minute: show the group split and one controlled comparison from metrics.csv.
State the test support, model, threshold, AP/recall and relevant uncertainty. Use
actual values from the delivered outputs; this guide supplies no invented score.

Final minute: explain what remains unresolved: surveillance selection, incomplete
metadata/cluster relationships, label-standard differences and external validity.
Describe one concrete next experiment and how you would avoid reusing the test
set to select its design.
